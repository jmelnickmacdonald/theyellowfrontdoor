A private look

More than a stack of listing graphics.

Good real estate marketing has a lot of jobs to do. Sometimes it needs to make one property impossible to scroll past. Sometimes it needs to make an agent's value easier to understand. And sometimes it needs to quietly make the client experience feel more organized.

The work below moves between all three. Some pieces were built for active clients. Lydia Nichols is a fictional brand we created so we could show the full range without borrowing someone else's business.

A complete fictional agent brand

Lydia Nichols from first impression to client follow-up.

Lydia started as a way to answer a simple question: what could an agent's marketing look like if the brand, the words, and the client experience were all built together? The answer became much bigger than a logo.

Business card designed for cream stock and gold foil.

One brand, a lot of useful places to take it.

Brand Identity · Print · Email · Buyer Experience · Referral Flow · Open House System

The visual pieces matter, but the strongest part of Lydia is what happens after someone pays attention. Her referral email has somewhere useful to send people. Her buyer guide gives clients one calm place to start. Her open house system helps turn a good event into thoughtful follow-up instead of a messy list of names.

It is all written in the same voice and built to feel like one agent is genuinely looking after the details.

Listing campaigns

The house is the same. The angle should not be.

A good listing campaign finds the part of the property people can actually picture themselves caring about. Sometimes that is the garage. Sometimes it is the space for a parent. Sometimes it is simply helping people see past an empty room.

A direct buyer callout built around a very specific wish list.

A lifestyle-led way into a property story.

Leading with the feature that deserved the attention.

Helping buyers see what an empty room could become.

A small piece of proof that the writing and presentation stood out.

Creative listing concept

Keys + Chemistry

Concept · Social Carousel · Listing Relaunch

This one treated a listing like a dating profile. Slightly cheeky, still useful, and a much better fit for a property that needed a fresh way back into the conversation.

The point was not to be cute for the sake of it. It was to make the details easier to notice and give people a reason to keep swiping.

Agent positioning

When the agent has a real point of difference, the marketing should make it obvious.

Kendra's previous experience with Edmonton Police Service is not a small biography detail. It gives her a genuine understanding of the schedules, pressure, and privacy concerns first responders bring into a move. The campaign makes that useful without turning it into a gimmick.

Real estate designed for first responders.

Positioning · Long-Form Copy · Print Design

The copy is practical on purpose. Flexible scheduling. Clear communication. Discretion. One person coordinating the moving parts. It speaks to the reality of the job instead of piling on generic claims about service.

That makes the piece feel credible because it comes from lived experience, not a marketing brainstorm.

Recruitment campaign

Building a smaller team on purpose.

This campaign was written for agents who are ready to build something with more care than a wide-open recruiting pitch. It talks about standards, ownership, and long-term fit because those are the things Kendra actually wanted to protect.

Print and event marketing

Sunset at Diamond Ridge

Campaign Concept · Postcard · Event Copy · Development Marketing

A development event could have been advertised as a list of showhomes and a date. Instead, this one was built around the feeling of seeing the foothills at sunset, meeting local makers, and imagining what could be built there.

The practical details are still easy to find. They just are not the only reason to care.
