Brand Direction

A first look at Farmhouse & Fields

The colours, type, photography, voice, and a few lines we can start building from. The goal is simple: when someone lands on the site, they should understand what the Farmhouse is, how it works, and why people trust Melissa with their dogs.

Prepared for Farmhouse & Fields • September 2026 • The Yellow Front Door

1 What Makes Farmhouse & Fields Special

The Farmhouse already has a very clear feel to it. The website does not need to invent one. It needs to show what Melissa has built: a small, consistent group of dogs, a place that feels comfortable and familiar, lots of room to play, and someone who knows and loves the dogs like her own.

At the Centre

Low, consistent numbers. Familiar dogs. Lots of room.

Farmhouse & Fields keeps the group small and familiar. Melissa gets to know each dog incredibly well: who they play with, how they settle in, what they love, and when they need a little space.

Melissa Knows These Dogs

That comes through all over her Facebook page. She knows their friendships, routines, quirks and personalities, and she clearly loves having them there.

That connection is one of the strongest parts of the business and it should be visible.

The Farmhouse Itself

The house, yard, barn and fields are part of the experience. It is the "home away from home" for these pups and is a big part of what makes the experience unique.

Warm and Easy

People should feel welcome here, but they should also be able to find what they need without digging: how daycare works, what the day looks like, pricing, the wait-list, policies, and what happens next.

In Practice: Red & Ink

  • Red for key moments: Buttons, links, small accents, and the occasional detail we want people to notice first.
  • Ink for reading: Navigation, body copy, forms, pricing, instructions, and anything that needs to be immediately clear.

In Practice: Blue, Oat & Photos

  • Blue and oat in supporting roles: Useful for quieter backgrounds, small sections, cards, or details. Neither needs to compete with the red.
  • Let the photos bring colour: We do not need to force every image into the palette or make the whole site look "rustic." The real place already does that.

How We Use Fraunces & DM Sans

  • Fraunces for headings: Page titles, section headings, and short lines where a little personality helps. Warm and distinctive without turning the site into a "country" theme.
  • DM Sans for the rest: Anything people need to scan, understand, fill out, or click.

Script Logo & Editable Guidelines

  • Let the logo keep its script: We do not know the exact script from the low-resolution logo file we have. For now, keep script inside the logo rather than adding another handwritten font.
  • Keep it simple in Canva: The editable text in this guide is set to Fraunces and DM Sans. Once we have the original logo file, we can see whether its script can be identified.

4 Photography

Her photos are one of the best parts of the brand. Melissa is fabulous at capturing the dogs as they actually are. Her Facebook page already tells the story of the Farmhouse through play, friendships, seasons, funny moments, the property, and the dogs' individual personalities. We should use that. So rich and fun and brings in an amazing storytelling element.

1. Use real photos throughout

Not just in a gallery at the end, but work them into the home page, About page, daycare information, and anywhere they help show what a day actually looks like.

2. Let the photos tell stories

Use the muddy ones, the goofy ones, the beautiful ones, group shots, quiet moments, and dogs with their favourite friends. They do not all need to look polished.

3. Keep editing light (but primarily zero)

Crop for layout and make small corrections when needed, but everything else stays the same.

4. No stock photos, ever

Tons of amazing photos makes the job so much easier. Use the photos throughout the site, build a standalone gallery, and pull in a box from Facebook for the day-to-day stories.

5 Voice

The website should sound like Melissa. Her Facebook page already has the right voice. It is warm, direct, and full of little stories because she actually knows these dogs—their friends, their habits, their quirks, what they love, and what makes them themselves. She clearly loves what she does, and that should come through without dressing it up.

What We Carry Into The Website

Specific dogs, real moments and down to earth language. A little humour when the moment calls for it. There is so much material to weave a beautiful little story.

Melissa knows and loves these dogs like her own. We want that to come through in the stories and details, not in a pile of marketing words.

What We Avoid

No lofty pet-care language, no trying to sound luxe, and no endless dog puns.

Skip words like "premium," "curated," and "exceptional" when plain language says it better.

1. Tell real stories

Her Facebook works because she tells us what actually happened: who found a friend, who lives for the pool, who came in ready to wrestle, or who had a very strong opinion about a costume.

2. Let the connection show

Melissa knows and loves these dogs like her own. We do not need to announce that on every page; the stories and specifics should make it obvious.

3. Keep the language normal

Warm and confident, but never lofty.

4. Be clear when it matters

Pricing, hours, policies, wait-list information, and next steps can simply be written plainly.

7 Keeping It Consistent

A few things worth keeping consistent. Nothing here needs to be complicated. These are just the choices that give us a solid starting point for the website and anything Melissa makes later.

1. Keep the site light: White first, with red as the strongest accent. Let the photos carry a lot of the colour.

2. Use the real place: Farmhouse, yard, barn, fields, seasons, and dogs. We don't need extra "farmhouse" decoration.

3. Keep type simple: Fraunces for headings, DM Sans for everything people need to read quickly. Let the logo keep its own script.

4. Use real photos only: Build from Melissa's gallery.

5. Keep the words warm and real: Specific stories, normal language, and no lofty pet-care copy.

6. Make the practical stuff easy: Pricing, hours, services, wait-list, policies, and next steps should always be easy to find.

8 Website Direction & Potential Pages

How the website could come together so Farmhouse & Fields feels like itself: Melissa, the dogs, the Farmhouse, and the photos. White stays dominant, the practical information stays easy to find, and the warmer storytelling has room to live throughout. The personality can live in the photos, writing, and details on each page. The navigation itself can stay very simple.

Page Title Content & Purpose
1. Home A first look at the Farmhouse, the daycare, and what makes it special, with great photos and clear ways to explore the rest of the site.
2. The Farmhouse Melissa's story, the house, yard, barn, and fields, and a little more about how Farmhouse & Fields came to be.
3. Daycare How daycare works, hours, pricing, group size, routines, requirements, and what families should know before their dog starts.
4. Gallery A home for Melissa's photography: everyday play, friendships, quiet moments, muddy days, changing seasons, and life around the Farmhouse.
5. FAQs Straightforward answers to the things families are most likely to wonder about before getting in touch.
6. Join the Wait-list A clear explanation of the process, what happens next, and a simple way for families to tell Melissa about their dog.
7. Current Clients An easy route to Time To Pet and any other information existing families need regularly. Can sit separately in navigation as a quick link.
8. Contact A simple way to reach Melissa.
